The lighthouse at Harrow Point had been automated for eleven years, which was why nobody questioned Odalys when she climbed its stairs every evening at dusk. There was nothing to tend. A sensor woke the lamp when the light failed, and a small gray box in the basement did the rest.

She climbed anyway. Ninety-two steps, her hand on the rail worn smooth by three generations of keepers before her. At the top she would stand beside the great lens, dark and enormous as a sleeping animal, and wait.

"You know it doesn't need you," the harbor inspector told her once, kindly, the way you speak to someone who is lost.

"I know," she said.

What she did not say was that the lamp had never needed anyone. It had needed *attention*, which is a different thing. A flame left alone drifts. A lens left alone clouds. Somebody had to be the person who noticed.

---

On the first night of the great storm, the sensor failed. Nobody ever found out why. The box in the basement sat blinking its small green blink, certain that the sky was still bright, and out past the rocks the ferry from Carrow Island turned toward a coastline that had gone entirely black.

Odalys was already at the top of the stairs.

She did not remember crossing the room. She remembered the cold brass of the manual switch, the stubborn old click of it, and then the lens waking up around her, a slow gold sunrise swinging out over the water.

Out in the dark, a ship's horn answered. Two long notes.

She stood there until the weather broke, one hand on the glass, not tending anything at all.
